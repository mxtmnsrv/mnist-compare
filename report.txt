This project focuses on evaluating a Multi-Layer Perceptron and a Convolutional Neural Network using both a standard version of the MNIST digit dataset and a specialized version where labels have been completely randomized. By creating a modular framework that supports data preparation, training, and visualization, we were able to observe how different model architectures handle spatial information and how they respond when the relationship between images and their categories is broken.

Our experiments demonstrated that the Convolutional Neural Network achieved a high validation accuracy of nearly ninety-nine percent on the original dataset while the simpler neural network reached approximately ninety-six percent under the same conditions. In stark contrast, both models failed to learn any meaningful patterns from the cursed dataset as evidenced by their validation accuracy staying near the ten percent mark which reflects the statistical probability of random guessing across ten digit classes.

We concluded that while advanced model architectures like convolutional layers provide a significant advantage in accuracy for image recognition tasks, the integrity of the underlying data labels is the most critical factor for successful learning. The failure of the models to generalize on the shuffled dataset proves that artificial intelligence requires a consistent and logical relationship between inputs and outputs to move beyond simple memorization and achieve true predictive power.
